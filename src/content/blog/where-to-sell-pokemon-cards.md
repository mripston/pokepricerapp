**The best place to sell Pokémon cards depends on what each card is worth.** Low-value bulk is quickest to sell to a local shop or bulk buyer, singles worth a few dollars or more usually do best on eBay or TCGplayer, and high-end cards are often worth sending to a specialist auction house.

Most people lose money by picking one outlet for everything. Sort first, then match each pile to the place that suits it.

## Sort your cards by value first

Before you choose where to sell, you need a rough price for each card. Check the set symbol, card number and finish, because a holofoil and a reverse holofoil of the same card can be priced very differently. If you have not done this before, start with [how to find out what Pokémon cards are worth](/blog/how-to-find-out-what-pokemon-cards-are-worth/).

Then split the collection into four piles:

| Pile | Rough value per card | Best places to sell |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bulk | Under $1 | Local card shop, online bulk buyers, lots on eBay or Facebook |
| Low singles | $1 to $20 | TCGplayer, eBay, local groups, card shows |
| Mid singles | $20 to a few hundred | eBay, TCGplayer, collector groups |
| High end | Many hundreds and up | eBay with authentication, specialist auction houses |

The dollar lines are not strict. They are just a way to stop you spending twenty minutes listing a card that sells for less than the postage.

## Online marketplaces: best price, most work

**eBay** has the biggest audience and suits almost any card. You can run an auction or set a fixed price, and the sold listings filter shows what buyers have actually paid recently. You take the photos, write the listing, pay selling fees and ship the card yourself. For expensive cards, eBay runs an authentication service on eligible listings, which gives buyers more confidence.

**TCGplayer** is built for trading cards. You list against an existing catalogue entry, so you do not need to write a description or take photos for ordinary cards. It works well if you have lots of singles in the low to mid range. Seller accounts are aimed at people in the United States.

**Cardmarket** fills the same role in Europe. If you live in the UK or the EU, it is usually the first place collectors look for singles.

On all three, remember that the price you see is not what you keep. Fees, postage and packing materials all come off the top, and on a cheap card they can take most of the sale.

## Local card shops and card shows: fast cash, lower price

A local card shop will look through your cards and make an offer on the spot. You get paid the same day and never have to ship anything. The trade-off is the price. A shop has to resell at a profit, so cash offers are usually well below market value, often around half for cards the shop actually wants. Store credit offers are normally higher, which is handy if you are still collecting.

Card shows work in a similar way, but with many dealers in one room. You can take the same cards to several tables and compare offers, which usually gets you a better number than a single shop visit. Bring your cards sleeved and sorted, with your best ones separate.

## Social media and local selling

Facebook Marketplace, Facebook collector groups, Reddit trading communities and Discord servers let you sell directly to other collectors with little or no selling fee. Prices can be close to market value because the buyer saves money too.

The risk is higher, though. Use payment methods that offer protection for goods, never accept an overpayment, and meet local buyers in a public place. Many groups keep a feedback thread, so check a buyer's history before you post a card.

## What to do with bulk

Commons, uncommons and basic energy rarely sell one at a time. Your options are:

1. Sell to a shop or online bulk buyer, who will pay a flat rate per thousand cards.
2. Bundle them into lots by set, type or Pokémon and list those on eBay or locally. Themed lots tend to sell better than random ones.
3. Donate them to a school club, a library or a young collector.

Before you hand over bulk, flip through it once more for holos, reverse holos and older cards. Not sure what counts? Here is [how to tell if a Pokémon card is rare](/blog/how-to-tell-if-a-pokemon-card-is-rare/).

## High-value cards need extra care

For a card worth serious money, slow down. Take clear photos of the front, back and corners, and describe the condition honestly. Ship with tracking and insurance. Specialist auction houses that handle trading cards take a commission, but they bring serious bidders and deal with payment and authentication for you. Many of them prefer graded cards, so ask before you send anything.

## Know your prices before you list

Whichever route you choose, a seller who knows the market price gets better offers and spots lowball ones quickly.
