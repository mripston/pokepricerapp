A **Pokémon card condition guide** comes down to five grades used for raw cards: Near Mint, Lightly Played, Moderately Played, Heavily Played and Damaged. The grade depends on wear in four places, the corners, edges, surface and whether the card has any bends or creases, and it has a big effect on what a card sells for.

## The five condition grades

These are the names used on TCGplayer and by most sellers in the US. Shops shorten them to NM, LP, MP, HP and DMG.

| Condition | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Near Mint (NM) | Looks close to pack fresh. At most a tiny speck of whitening or a faint surface mark you have to hunt for. |
| Lightly Played (LP) | Minor wear you can see on a close look: light whitening on a few edges or corners, a few light scratches. No creases or bends. |
| Moderately Played (MP) | Wear you can see straight away: whitening around most of the border, scuffing, scratches, maybe a slight bend or minor dent. |
| Heavily Played (HP) | Heavy wear all over: worn corners, lots of whitening, deep scratches, light creases. Still in one piece and still sleeves up fine. |
| Damaged (DMG) | Something structural is wrong: deep creases, tears, peeling, water damage, writing or ink, or bends that will not lie flat. |

When a card sits between two grades, list it as the lower one. Buyers rarely complain about a card that arrives better than described.

## How to check a card yourself

You do not need special tools. A bright lamp or a window and a clean, dark surface are enough.

1. **Start with the back.** The blue border shows white specks and chipped edges far more clearly than the front does. Most whitening lives here.
2. **Look at all four corners.** Sharp and square is good. Soft, rounded, bent or peeling corners each push the grade down.
3. **Run your eye along the edges.** Look for nicks, rough patches and whitening on both sides.
4. **Tilt the card under the light.** Scratches, scuffs and dents on the surface only show at an angle. Holo foil scratches very easily, so check the artwork box carefully.
5. **Check for bends and creases.** Hold the card flat at eye level and look across it, then tilt the back in the light. A crease shows up as a line that catches the light.

Handle cards by the edges while you do this, and put anything worth keeping straight into a sleeve.

## What moves a card down a grade

Some flaws matter more than others:

- **Creases and bends** do the most harm. Even one small crease takes a card out of Near Mint and Lightly Played entirely.
- **Surface scratches on holo foil** are the most common reason an otherwise clean older card ends up Lightly or Moderately Played.
- **Edge and corner whitening** is expected on played cards. A speck or two can still be Near Mint. Whitening around the whole border cannot.
- **Stains, ink, stickers and water damage** generally mean Damaged, however good the rest of the card looks.
- **Factory flaws** such as print lines and off-centre borders are a grey area. Marketplaces mostly judge wear rather than centering for raw cards, but obvious print defects should still be mentioned in a listing.

A card that has been played without sleeves is almost never Near Mint. A card that went from pack to sleeve to binder usually is.

## Cardmarket uses a different scale

If you buy or sell in Europe, Cardmarket has seven grades instead of five: Mint, Near Mint, Excellent, Good, Light Played, Played and Poor. The names do not line up neatly with the US ones. Excellent on Cardmarket is roughly where Lightly Played sits in the US, and Good is a noticeably worn card, not a nice one. Read the marketplace's own descriptions before you list, because "Good" catches a lot of new sellers out.

## How condition affects price

Price guides and market prices are usually based on cards in Near Mint or close to it. Each step down takes a slice off. Lightly Played copies tend to sell at a modest discount, Moderately Played at a clear one, and Heavily Played or Damaged copies often go for a fraction of the Near Mint price.

How steep the drop is depends on the card. Modern cards are easy to find in Near Mint, so buyers have little reason to pay much for a worn copy. Vintage cards are harder to find clean, so played copies of popular old holos hold up better in relative terms. Either way, be honest with yourself about condition before you get excited about a number. ## Condition is not the same as grading

Near Mint, Lightly Played and the rest describe raw cards, and the seller decides the label. Grading is a separate service where a company such as PSA, BGS or CGC inspects the card, gives it a number from 1 to 10 and seals it in a plastic slab. A raw Near Mint card is not guaranteed a high grade, since graders also look hard at centering and tiny surface flaws.

For most cards, a fair raw condition and a current market price are all you need.
