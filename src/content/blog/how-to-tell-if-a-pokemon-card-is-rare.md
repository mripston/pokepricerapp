To tell if a Pokémon card is rare, look at the small symbol printed next to the card number in the bottom corner. A **circle** means common, a **diamond** means uncommon and a **star** means rare. Everything else is a variation on that system.

## The three basic rarity symbols

Every English Pokémon card from the main sets carries one of these:

| Symbol | Rarity | How often you see it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ● Circle | Common | Several in every pack |
| ◆ Diamond | Uncommon | A few in every pack |
| ★ Star | Rare | Usually one per pack |

On cards from the first two decades of the game, the symbol sits in the bottom right corner. On most modern cards it has moved to the bottom left, beside the set code and card number.

## Modern cards use more than one star

Starting with the Scarlet & Violet series, the star was expanded into a small ladder so you can read the rarity tier straight off the card:

- **One black star:** rare
- **Two black stars:** double rare, used for most Pokémon ex
- **Two silver stars:** ultra rare, typically full art cards
- **One gold star:** illustration rare
- **Two gold stars:** special illustration rare
- **Three gold stars:** hyper rare, the gold cards

If your card has gold stars, it came from one of the hardest slots in the pack to hit.

## Check the card number for secret rares

The card number is written as two figures, such as 86/110. The second figure is the size of the main set.

When the first figure is **higher** than the second, for example 149/147, the card is a secret rare. These sit outside the numbered set and are pulled far less often than ordinary rares. It is one of the quickest checks you can do, and it works on cards from almost every era.

## Other marks worth knowing

- **Black star with "PROMO":** a promotional card, given out through events, boxes or products rather than booster packs. Some are common, some are very hard to find.
- **1st Edition stamp:** a small black circle with a "1" on early sets. It marks the first print run and can change the price dramatically.
- **Holofoil artwork:** a shiny picture box generally means the card is at least a rare, although reverse holofoil versions exist for commons and uncommons too.

## Rare does not always mean valuable

Rarity tells you how often a card comes out of a pack. Price is set by how many people want it.

A plain one-star rare from a recent set is often worth less than a dollar because so many were opened. Meanwhile, a common from a 1999 set in clean condition can sell for more than that, simply because few survived in good shape. Popular Pokémon, striking artwork and competitive play all move prices in ways the rarity symbol cannot show.

So use the symbol as a first filter. Pull out anything with a star, anything with a number above the set total and anything with a 1st Edition stamp, and then check what those cards actually sell for.

## A quick sorting routine

1. Split the pile by symbol: circles, diamonds, stars.
2. From the stars, pull out anything holofoil, full art or with gold or silver stars.
3. Check every card number for secret rares.
4. Set aside anything from older sets regardless of symbol.
5. Look up prices for the cards you set aside.

That last step is where a scanner saves the most time. Rather than typing each card name, you can scan the stack and let the prices tell you which cards deserve a sleeve.
