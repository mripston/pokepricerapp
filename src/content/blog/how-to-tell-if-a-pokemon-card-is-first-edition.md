To tell if a Pokémon card is first edition, look for the **1st Edition stamp**: a small black circle with a "1" in the middle and the word "EDITION" curved over the top. On a Pokémon card it sits on the left, just below the bottom left corner of the artwork. If the stamp is not there, the card is not 1st Edition, no matter how old it is.

## What the 1st Edition stamp looks like

The stamp is small, a little larger than the rarity symbol, and printed in solid black ink. It has three parts:

- a black circle
- a "1" inside the circle
- the word "EDITION" arched above it

Where it sits depends on the type of card:

| Card type | Where to look |
| --- | --- |
| Pokémon cards | Left side, just below the bottom left corner of the picture |
| Trainer cards | Bottom left corner of the card |

If you are sorting a pile, check both spots on every card. It only takes a second.

## Which sets have 1st Edition cards

In English, only the early sets printed by Wizards of the Coast had a 1st Edition run. That covers ten sets, released between 1999 and 2002:

1. Base Set
2. Jungle
3. Fossil
4. Team Rocket
5. Gym Heroes
6. Gym Challenge
7. Neo Genesis
8. Neo Discovery
9. Neo Revelation
10. Neo Destiny

Each of these had a first run with the stamp, then a larger Unlimited run without it.

Sets from the same era that never had a 1st Edition print include Base Set 2 and Legendary Collection. After Neo Destiny, English sets dropped the stamp altogether, so a modern English card with a 1st Edition stamp is a warning sign.

Japanese cards are a separate story. Japanese sets used their own 1st Edition mark for many years after English sets stopped, so a stamp on a much newer Japanese card can be perfectly normal.

## 1st Edition, Shadowless and Unlimited in Base Set

Base Set is the one place this gets confusing, because it has three main versions rather than two:

- **1st Edition:** has the stamp, and has no drop shadow on the right side of the artwork frame.
- **Shadowless:** no stamp, but still no drop shadow. This was a short print run right after 1st Edition.
- **Unlimited:** no stamp, with a dark drop shadow along the right and bottom edge of the artwork frame. This is the version most people have.

The practical point: a genuine 1st Edition Base Set card is always shadowless. If a Base Set card has the stamp and also has the shadow beside the picture, treat it with suspicion.

There is one well-known exception. The holofoil Machamp from the Base Set starter deck carries a 1st Edition stamp on every copy, including the later shadowed ones. The stamp is real, but because every copy has it, it does not make the card scarce.

## How to spot a fake 1st Edition stamp

Because the stamp can change a card's price so much, people do add fake stamps to ordinary Unlimited cards. A few checks catch most of them:

1. **Check the set.** If the card is not from one of the ten sets above, an English 1st Edition stamp should not be there.
2. **Check for the shadow.** On Base Set cards, a stamp plus a drop shadow is a red flag, Machamp aside.
3. **Look at the ink.** A real stamp is crisp, evenly black and sits flat as part of the card's print. Fuzzy edges, a grey or glossy look, or ink that seems to sit on top of the surface all point to a stamp added later.
4. **Compare the position and size.** Put the card next to a photo of a confirmed 1st Edition copy of the same card. Fake stamps are often slightly too big, too thick or tilted.
5. **Check the rest of the card.** A fake stamp is sometimes on a fully fake card. Our guide on [how to tell if a Pokémon card is rare](/blog/how-to-tell-if-a-pokemon-card-is-rare/) covers the symbols and numbers a real card should have.

For an expensive card, buying a copy already authenticated by a grading company removes most of the guesswork.

## Does the stamp make a card valuable?

Usually it makes it worth more than the same card without the stamp, sometimes many times more. 1st Edition runs were smaller, and fewer of those cards survived in good shape.

That does not mean every stamped card is a big find. A 1st Edition common that has been through a playground will often sell for a modest amount, while a clean holofoil of a popular Pokémon can be worth a great deal. Condition matters a lot with cards this old, so look closely at corners, edges and surface scratches before you get excited.

## Checking what your 1st Edition card sells for

When you look up a price, make sure you are looking at the 1st Edition listing and not the Unlimited one. They are priced separately, and mixing them up is the most common mistake people make with old cards.
